Export to Fabric

Export to Fabric publishes a model's Report as a Fabric app. People open it in the browser after signing in to Fabric, and use its tiles and slicers there with nothing to install.

It is experimental, and it will change

Export to Fabric is an experiment. It works today, but it is not finished, and you should not build on it yet.

  • It is built on Fabric apps, which is new and still changing. When Fabric apps changes, the export and the apps it makes change with it.
  • What the export asks, what the app looks like, the .fabricator file, and how the app signs in and reads its data can all change from one version of OneLake Studio to the next.
  • An app you publish today may need to be exported again after an update, and may stop working until you do.
  • Numbers computed from a semantic model aren't guaranteed to match Power BI.

Export to Fabric asks before it writes anything. SQL can write to a lakehouse too, once you turn it on: see Write to OneLake.

What it publishes

The export publishes the Report page of a model, as it is, with the Fabric REST API. Only people who can open the app in Fabric can load it.

The app is also saved on your computer as one .fabricator file. It is a Fabricator project: with Fabricator installed, double-click it to keep building the app there and publish it again, to the same app. OneLake Studio opens it too, on its Report page, from File › Open or by right-clicking it in VS Code.

How the app reads

The app is made from a model file, an Ossie .yaml or a .bim. DuckDB runs in the browser and reads the lakehouse's tables. The app's function creates a read-only link for each table in the model, and for no other table.

The workspace must allow OneLake user-delegated SAS tokens. The tables are read with the rights of the app's owner, so everyone who can open the app sees what its owner sees.
